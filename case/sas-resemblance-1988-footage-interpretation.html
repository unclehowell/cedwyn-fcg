Status: INTERPRETATION OF THE EVIDENCE. This page reads what the footage shows and where it sits in the record. It names no one and makes no identification.

What the footage shows

The ITV Wales (then HTV Wales) news report of the 1988 possession enforcement at Great House Farm relays neighbours likening the repossession to an SAS raid, and the report itself describes the eviction as “SAS style” (EV-0083 — ITV/HTV Wales report of the eviction and injunction).

What we observe

The people shown acting as BP’s bailiffs bear a striking resemblance to SAS soldiers in their appearance, kit and manner. That is an observation of the footage, set beside the report’s own words. It is not a finding about anyone’s identity, service or employer. The record of who the people shown were is still being sought (EV-0082 — identity and role of the people in the footage).

The wider context

  • Late release. The viewing copy of the footage was first published online on 25 July 2025, after a June 2025 email that sought disclosure and raised concealment (ITV Cymru Wales — Forensic Assessment).
  • Broadcast not confirmed. ITV Wales has neither confirmed nor denied whether the footage aired. The National Library of Wales lists item 507738, “Eviction News Material, 30 November 1988”, as “Not viewed” (EV-0379 — ITV archive search request; EV-0418 — National Library of Wales footage access enquiry).
  • The “Welsh Crichel Down”. In November 1958 the Western Mail reported the farm’s position as a Welsh Crichel Down, the post-war case on land taken by government (EV-0037 — Western Mail, 20 November 1958).
  • War Agricultural Executive Committee. Wartime agricultural and military requisition at Llandough, the Crichel Down rules, and the Ministry of Agriculture files still to be read (EV-0622 — Crichel Down rules and military land).
  • Notice of the 1983 registration. HM Land Registry’s notice of the 1983 first registration reached the Williams family only after the window to object or appeal had closed (EV-0060 — HM Land Registry notification received after Mary (Nancy) Williams’s death; 1982–83 Registration & Opportunity to Object).
  • The 1982 / 1983 discrepancy. The 1987 Court of Appeal judgment gives the registration as November 1982: “since November 1982 they have been the registered proprietors of it at HM Land Registry following first registration” (Dillon LJ). The first registration on record is the 1983 one (EV-0080 — BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2; 1983 First Registration: Parcel A Title Basis).
  • Other points of procedural unfairness and court error already on this site: Procedural Fairness & Notice; 1987 HMLR Amalgamation During Appeal; Limitation & Procedural Deadline Firewall; Court of Appeal — Forensic Assessment; Concealment, Missing Records & Disclosure.

Our reading

Read together, the footage, its late release, the broadcaster’s silence on whether it aired, the 1958 Crichel Down report, the wartime agricultural record, the timing of the 1983 notice and the judgment’s 1982 date increase the likelihood of concealment and of government involvement in how the farm was taken. That is our interpretation of the evidence. Each point links to its own record, so the reader can weigh it.

What would settle it

The full or highest-generation broadcast copy and its transmission log; National Library of Wales item 507738; and the enforcement records naming who carried out the possession and for whom. Any new record is added as its own evidence page and linked here, including anything that cuts against this reading.
