# DRAFT chapter for the next FCG edition — for the owner's review, not published

**The threshold: what would reopen the case, and what is still missing**

> Not legal advice. This chapter is a research framework. The legal tests are summarised from general knowledge of the law of England and Wales and each one needs checking by a solicitor or barrister before anyone relies on it. Data behind it: `drafts/threshold-matrix.json`.

## Why this chapter exists

A court does not reopen a decided case because a history looks wrong. It reopens one when a party shows particular things, each with a record. This chapter sets out what those things are, which of them the archive can show today, and which it cannot. Where a record points the other way, it is listed beside the rest.

## What the courts decided, and what they did not

On 31 July 1987 the Court of Appeal decided possession for the registered proprietor [EV-0080]. Its reasoning rests on two things: a possession order of 11 December 1962 [EV-0041], and letters of 31 October 1974 which the court treated as turning the occupation into a licence [EV-0050, EV-0074]. The court recorded that the company had “the paper title” and had been registered proprietor since November 1982. It did not try ownership of the house as a question of title, and no decision on Mary Williams's plea of ownership of 3 July 1974 has been found [EV-0049].

Anything that reopens the case has to bear on one of those two foundations, or on the registration itself.

## The routes, element by element

**Route A: setting a judgment aside because it was obtained by dishonesty.** Three things are needed: deliberate dishonesty by a party; that it caused the decision; and evidence of both that the court did not have. *Held today:* the judgment, which shows what the decision turned on. *Not held:* the affidavits, pleadings and disclosure lists of 1984–87, and the first-registration applications. *Reading:* no record held shows a statement that its maker knew to be untrue. A document that was not produced in 1987 only helps here if it shows that a party knew something different from what it told the court.

**Route B: more time because a fact was deliberately kept back (Limitation Act 1980, s.32).** The fact must be one the other side knew and chose not to disclose; silence or carelessness is not enough. *Held:* the dated sequence of first registration and of the amalgamation of 2 February 1987 [EV-0059, EV-0066, EV-0065, EV-0078]. *Not held:* any record of notice to the occupier at either step. *Against:* registration is public, and the 1987 judgment itself records it. The same section protects buyers for value, and the site was sold in 1993 and built on.

**Route C: a mistake in the register, and indemnity (Land Registration Act 2002, Schedules 4 and 8).** The question is whether land was registered that the applicant's deeds did not carry. That is decided by laying plans side by side: the conveyance of 25 December 1938 [EV-0216, recited only], the conveyance of 31 December 1969 [EV-0043, recited only], the tenancy plan of 18 May 1939 [EV-0023, held: it shows fields and not the farmhouse], and the registered extent. Three of those four plans are not held. *Against:* the registry's reply of May 2026 that the title was registered correctly [EV-0352]; the tithe and poor-rate books, which enter the Marquess of Bute as owner [EV-0215, EV-0212]. The present owners are in possession and bought later, so the realistic remedy on this route is indemnity, and the time limit for that runs from when the claimant knew or could have known.

**Route D: findings by ombudsmen and the Information Commissioner.** Each needs a dated request, a reply and the rule the reply did not meet. These are documentable now (see That Old Chestnut). They produce findings and disclosure. They do not reopen title.

**Two points that come before every route.** First, standing: who now holds whatever right Mary Williams and William Buckler had. Second, the root of title. The family's central working theory is that the Williams family held the house as freeholders. The records that name the holding before 1900 enter John Williams as occupier and Bute as owner [EV-0211, EV-0215, EV-0212], and the court recited a yearly tenancy of 1916 [EV-0014]. On 13 November 2026 Glamorgan Archives reported that a search of its catalogues found no lease of Great House of about 1660–75, no later deed reciting one, and no conveyance of Great House from the Vaughan family to Bute or Windsor. That was a search of catalogues, not of documents.

## Where this leaves the threshold

The records held today do not fill any element of Route A or Route B. Route C can be decided by documents, and most of those documents are not yet held. Route D is open now. The pre-tithe records named above have not been read by anyone on the project and can go either way: they are as able to show a Bute or Vaughan proprietor as a Williams one. A gap in the record is not proof that anything was hidden.
